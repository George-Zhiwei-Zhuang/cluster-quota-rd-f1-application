# Section 11 revision notes for the rebuilt package

These notes map the frozen, rule-based rebuild to Section 11 of
`Cluster_Based_RDD_Rewritten(20260808-123526).docx`. They are not a replacement
for the generated tables and figures; those files remain the numerical source
of record.

## 11.1 Institutional setting and data

Replace the reported 192 weekends and 2,994 Q2 driver-race observations with
193 weekends and 3,010 observations. The raw snapshot contains 198 audited
weekends. Five are excluded under explicit rules:

- 2011 Indian GP: ranks 10 and 11 have equal recorded Q2 times;
- 2015 United States GP: Q3 was not held;
- 2016 Australian and Bahrain GPs: the temporary elimination format advanced
  eight rather than ten drivers to Q3;
- 2018 Russian GP: only ten drivers recorded Q2 times, so a rank-11 Q2 score is
  unavailable.

Do not state that prior-season performance is set to zero for rookies in the
main analysis. Instead state that prior-season points and points per start use
the 130 focal races in which both drivers had at least one start in the prior
season. Report the no-prior-season-history contrast separately. The
zero-inclusive total-points contrast is retained only as a labeled sensitivity.

## 11.4 Score-gap distribution

The substantive result is unchanged. The gap ranges from 0.002 to 1.564
seconds; the median is 0.063. The 5th, 25th, 50th, 75th and 95th percentiles
are 0.007, 0.030, 0.063, 0.116 and 0.366 seconds. Figure 5 is regenerated from

## 11.5 Gap heterogeneity

Because 193 is not divisible by four, the gap quartiles contain 49, 48, 48 and
48 races. The race-point contrasts are 0.143, 0.146, 1.063 and 1.375. On the
complete-history samples, prior-season-points contrasts are -37.000, 7.182,
25.935 and 8.309; sample sizes are 32, 33, 31 and 34. This pattern does not
support the earlier claim of a systematic monotone predetermined imbalance.

In the continuous specification, a doubling of the gap is associated with
0.371 race points (p = 0.402), 0.025 in top-ten probability (p = 0.553),
12.747 complete-pair prior-season points (p = 0.097), and 0.679 complete-pair
prior-season points per start (p = 0.097). These remain descriptive
comparability diagnostics.

## 11.6 Boundary versus neighboring ranks

For race points, the focal contrast is 0.679, the nearest-neighbor mean is
0.482, and the boundary excess is 0.197 (season-cluster p = 0.670; sign-
enumeration p = 0.668). For top-ten finish, the corresponding values are
0.067, 0.039 and 0.028 (p = 0.563). The starting-grid boundary excess remains
large at -1.720 positions (p < 0.001).

The prior-season-points comparison must use one common complete-history sample
for ranks 9 through 12. On that sample (N = 83), the focal contrast is 9.904,
the nearest-neighbor mean is 8.639, and the boundary excess is 1.265
(p = 0.900). Pair-specific complete cases should not be compared because they
do not represent the same races.

## 11.7 Dependence and extreme-gap sensitivity

Removing the one, five, ten and twenty largest gaps produces race-point
contrasts of 0.698, 0.686, 0.623 and 0.532. The corresponding top-ten
contrasts are 0.068, 0.064, 0.049 and 0.040. Leave-one-season-out race-point
estimates remain positive and range from 0.500 to 0.823.

Accordingly, the descriptive contrast is not driven by one extreme race or
one season, but its magnitude declines when the comparison is restricted to
more score-local margins. The inference discussion should continue to stress
that all four interval constructions are sampling-based sensitivities rather
than design-based randomization intervals.
